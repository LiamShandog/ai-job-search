## Overview

No formal instrument has been administered. Based on Liam's self-description and
his track record, the working pattern is **high-ownership, high-drive, autonomy-seeking**:
he wants to be handed a hard problem and trusted to finish it, measures a job by
how much he learns and who he learns it from, and is most productive around
people he considers driven and technically strong. The recurring friction is
tolerance: for routine work, and for being treated as junior.

**No formal assessment on file.** If asked in an interview about working style,
answer from concrete examples in `07-interview-prep.md` rather than claiming a
personality type.

## Strongest Behaviors

- **Takes and finishes ownership.** Given the software stack for a 15-person team, he owns delegation, onboarding, and the build system rather than just his own tickets. Two paid roles delivered production systems that people depend on daily.
- **Works the software/hardware boundary.** Comfortable moving between a React front end, a Python pipeline, a ROS 2 node, and an Arduino motor driver. Genuinely unusual range for a third-year.
- **Translates for non-technical stakeholders.** Worked "hand-in-hand with employees across every division" of a construction company to turn dispatcher workflows into software. This is the core Forward Deployed Engineer competency and it is evidenced, not asserted.
- **Optimizes what he is not asked to optimize.** Cut recurring compute cost from \$100+/week to single digits by rearchitecting how data was queried. Nobody assigns that; it comes from noticing waste.
- **Composure under pressure with hostile stakeholders.** [Inferred from 2.5 years officiating hockey] Split-second rulings, conflict management, and holding authority over adults and coaches as a teenager.
- **Teaches.** Onboards new ROS 2 contributors; mentored first-year engineering students as a FREC; tutored grade 11 sciences and maths. Recurs across three unrelated contexts, so it is a real trait rather than an incident.

## How You Work Best

- Handed a hard, well-defined problem and trusted with the outcome, with minimal check-in
- On a team of people who are visibly driven and technically strong, where he is the most junior person by experience but not treated as the least capable
- Where the work has a visible user and a measurable result
- Across the full stack and across disciplines, rather than confined to one narrow layer
- Where autonomy is granted early and expands with demonstrated delivery

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)

- **Low tolerance for routine work.** Stated plainly: mundane tasks drain him. Real engineering roles, including elite ones, contain maintenance, migration, code review, documentation, and on-call. **Frame as:** drawn to problems with headroom, and honest that he does his best work where there is something to figure out. **Do not** say he dislikes mundane tasks in an interview; it reads as an unwillingness to do the unglamorous parts of the job, which every team has.
- **Impatience with less-driven colleagues.** "I do not love working with mundane employees." Understandable, and a liability if voiced. It also predicts friction in large organizations with mixed performance levels. **Frame as:** thrives in high-standards teams. **Never** phrase it as a judgment of former or prospective colleagues.
- **Sensitivity to being underestimated for his age.** A legitimate frustration and a real signal about which environments suit him. But raising it unprompted, or having it show as defensiveness when challenged technically, will cost him. **Frame as:** looking for a team that gives interns real ownership, and let the track record establish credibility rather than arguing for it.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a posting mentions these, it is a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "ownership", "own the outcome", "end-to-end responsibility", "autonomy"
- "ambiguous problems", "hard technical problems", "first principles", "0 to 1"
- "high-agency", "high bar", "exceptional teammates", "steep learning curve"
- "customer-facing engineer", "deployed with users", "forward deployed"
- "small team", "founding team", "high trust, low process"
- "interns ship to production", "real projects, not busywork"

When a posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not a deal-breaker):
- "maintenance", "legacy system support", "production support rotation", "sustainment"
- "highly structured environment", "well-defined processes", "follow established procedures"
- "large established team", "matrix organization", "extensive onboarding period"
- "documentation-heavy", "change control", "waterfall"
- Internship programs described mainly as training or rotation rather than delivery

## Management Style Preferences

- **Works for:** a manager who assigns outcomes rather than tasks, is technically strong enough to earn his respect, gives direct feedback, and expands scope as he delivers
- **Does not work for:** close supervision, task-level direction, deference gated on seniority, or a manager who treats an intern's judgment as automatically less valuable
